The Doctrine of Election

He then spoke on the subject of election, and read the 9th chapter of Romans,6 from which it was evident that the election there spoken of was pertaining to the flesh,7 and had reference to the seed of Abraham,8 according to the promise God made to Abraham, saying, “In thee, and in thy seed, all the families of the earth shall be blessed.”9 To them belonged the adoption and the covenants,10 &c. Paul said, when he saw their unbelief, “I wish myself accursed”11—according to the flesh—not according to the spirit. Why did God say to Pharaoh, “For this cause have I raised thee up”?12 Because Pharaoh was a fit instrument—a wicked man,13 and had committed acts of cruelty of the most atrocious nature. The election of the promised seed still continues, and in the last day, they shall have the Priesthood restored14 unto them, and they shall be the “saviors on Mount Zion,”15 the ministers of our God;16 if it were not for the remnant which was left, then might men now be as Sodom and Gomorrah.17 The whole of the chapter had reference to the Priesthood and the house of Israel;18 and unconditional election of individuals to eternal life was not taught by the Apostles. God did elect or predestinate,19 that all those who would be saved, should be saved in Christ Jesus,20 and through obedience to the Gospel;21 but He passes over no man’s sins,22 but visits them with correction, and if His children will not repent of their sins He will discard them.

This is an imperfect sketch of a very interesting discourse, which occupied more than two hours in delivery, and was listened to with marked attention, by the vast assembly present. (May 16, 1841.) DHC 4:358–360.

Three Independent Principles

May 16, 1841. There are three independent principles; the Spirit of God,23 the spirit of man,24 and the spirit of the devil.25 All men have power to resist the devil.26

After baptism, the congregation again repaired to the grove, near the Temple, to attend to the ordinance of confirmation,1 and, notwithstanding President Smith had spoken in the open air to the people, and stood in the water and baptized about eighty persons, about fifty of those baptized received their confirmation under his hands2 in the after part of the day. While this was progressing, great numbers were being baptized in the font for the dead.3 (March 20, 1842.) DHC 4:553–557.

Synopsis of the Prophet’s Sermon on Baptism for the Dead

This was an interesting day. A large assembly met in the grove near the Temple. Brother Amasa Lyman addressed the people in a very interesting manner. He was followed by Joseph, the Seer,4 who made some highly edifying and instructive remarks concerning baptism for the dead. He said the Bible supported the doctrine, quoting 1 Cor. 15:29: “Else what shall they do which are baptized for the dead, if the dead rise not at all, why are they then baptized for the dead?”5 If there is one word of the Lord that supports the doctrine of baptism for the dead, it is enough to establish it as a true doctrine. Again; if we can, by the authority of the Priesthood of the Son of God,6 baptize a man in the name of the Father, of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost,7 for the remission of sins, it is just as much our privilege to act as an agent, and be baptized for the remission of sins for and in behalf of our dead kindred, who have not heard the Gospel,8 or the fullness of it. (March 27, 1842.) DHC 4:568–569.

Synopsis of the Prophet’s Remarks to the Female Relief Society

It is my meditation all the day, and more than my meat and drink,1 to know how I shall make the Saints of God2 comprehend the visions that roll like an overflowing surge before my mind.3

* * *

All your losses will be made up to you in the resurrection,4 provided you continue faithful.5 By the vision of the Almighty6 I have seen it.

More painful to me are the thoughts of annihilation than death. If I have no expectation of seeing my father, mother, brothers, sisters and friends again, my heart would burst in a moment, and I should go down to my grave.7

The expectation of seeing my friends in the morning of the resurrection cheers my soul and makes me bear up against the evils of life. It is like their taking a long journey, and on their return we meet them with increased joy.8

God has revealed His Son from the heavens9 and the doctrine of the resurrection10 also; and we have a knowledge that those we bury here God will bring up again, clothed upon11 and quickened by the Spirit12 of the great God;13 and what mattereth it whether we lay them down, or we lay down with them when we can keep them no longer? Let these truths sink down in our hearts, that we may even here begin to enjoy that which shall be in full hereafter.14

Hosanna, hosanna, hosanna to Almighty God,15 that rays of light begin to burst forth upon us16 even now. I cannot find words in which to express myself. I am not learned,17 but I have as good feelings as any man.

O that I had the language of the archangel18 to express my feelings once to my friends! But I never expect to in this life. When others rejoice, I rejoice;19 when they mourn, I mourn.20

To Marcellus Bates let me administer comfort.21 You shall soon have the company of your companion in a world of glory,22 and the friends of Brother Barnes and all the Saints who are mourning. This has been a warning voice23 to us all to be sober24 and diligent and lay aside25 mirth,26 vanity27 and folly,28 and to be prepared to die tomorrow.29

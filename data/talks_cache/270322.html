STPJS 322

If I have sinned, I have sinned outwardly; but surely I have contemplated the things of God.1

Respecting the Melchizedek Priesthood, the sectarians never professed to have it; consequently they never could save any one, and would all be damned together.2 There was an Episcopal priest who said he had the priesthood of Aaron,3 but had not the priesthood of Melchizedek:4 and I bear testimony that I never have found the man who claimed the Priesthood of Melchizedek. The power of the Melchizedek Priesthood5 is to have the power of “endless6 lives”;7 for the everlasting covenant8 cannot be broken.9

The law was given under Aaron10 for the purpose of pouring out judgments11 and destructions.

* * *

Three Grand Orders

There are three grand orders of priesthood12 referred to here.

1st. The King of Shiloam (Salem)13 had power and authority over that of Abraham,14 holding the key and the power of endless life.15 Angels desire to look into it, but they have set up too many stakes.16 God cursed the children of Israel17 because they would not receive the last law from Moses.18

The sacrifice required of Abraham19 in the offering up of Isaac,20 shows that if a man would attain to the keys of the kingdom of an endless life;21 he must sacrifice all things.22 When God offers a blessing or knowledge to a man, and he refuses to receive it,23 he will be damned. The Israelites prayed that God would speak to Moses and not to them;24 in consequence of which he cursed them with a carnal law.25

What was the power of Melchizedek?26 ’Twas not the Priesthood of Aaron which administers in outward ordinances,27 and the offering of sacrifices.28 Those holding the fulness of the Melchizedek Priesthood29 are kings and priests30 of the Most High God,31 holding the keys of power and blessings.32 In fact, that Priesthood is a perfect law33 of theocracy, and stands as God to give laws to the people, administering34 endless35 lives36 to the sons and daughters of Adam.37

STPJS 217
Men are Saved Through Obedience to Knowledge

We have thieves among us, adulterers,1 liars, hypocrites.2 If God should speak from heaven,3 he would command you not to steal, not to commit adultery, not to covet, nor deceive,4 but be faithful over a few things.5 As far as we degenerate from God, we descend to the devil and lose knowledge,6 and without knowledge we cannot be saved,7 and while our hearts are filled with evil,8 and we are studying evil,9 there is no room in our hearts for good,10 or studying good. Is not God good?11 Then you be good;12 if He is faithful,13 then you be faithful.14 Add to your faith virtue, to virtue knowledge,15 and seek for every good thing.16

The Church must be cleansed,17 and I proclaim against all iniquity. A man is saved no faster than he gets knowledge,18 for if he does not get knowledge, he will be brought into captivity by some evil power19 in the other world, as evil spirits will have more knowledge, and consequently more power20 than many men who are on the earth. Hence it needs revelation to assist us, and give us knowledge of the things of God.21

What is the reason that the Priests of the day do not get revelation? They ask only to consume it upon their lusts.22 Their hearts are corrupt,23 and they cloak their iniquity by saying there are no more revelations.24 But if any revelations are given of God, they are universally opposed by the priests and Christendom at large; for they reveal their wickedness and abominations.25 (April 10, 1842.) DHC 4:588.

Baptism for the Dead

The great designs of God26 in relation to the salvation of the human family, are very little understood by the professedly wise and intelligent generation in which we live. Various and conflicting are the opinions of men concerning the plan of salvation, the requisitions of the Almighty, the necessary preparations for heaven, the state and condition of departed spirits,27 and the happiness or misery that is consequent upon the practice of righteousness and iniquity according to their several notions of virtue and vice.

The Mussulman condemns the heathen, the Jew, and the Christian, and the whole world of mankind that reject his Koran, as infidels,28 and consigns the whole of them to perdition.29

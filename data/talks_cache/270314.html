“Do you believe in the baptism of infants?”4 asks the Presbyterian. No. “Why?” Because it is nowhere written in the Bible. Circumcision is not baptism,5 neither was baptism instituted in the place of circumcision. Baptism is for remission of sins.6 Children have no sins.7 Jesus blessed them8 and said, “Do what you have seen me do.”9 Children are all made alive in Christ,10 and those of riper years through faith and repentance.11

So far we are agreed with other Christian denominations. They all preach faith and repentance.12 The gospel13 requires baptism by immersion for the remission of sins,14 which is the meaning of the word in the original language—namely, to bury or immerse.15

Necessity of Ordinances

We ask the sects, Do you believe this? They answer, No. I believe in being converted. I believe in this tenaciously. So did the Apostle Peter and the disciples of Jesus.16 But I further believe in the gift of the Holy Ghost by the laying on of hands.17 Evidence by Peter’s preaching on the day of Pentecost, Acts 2:38.18 You might as well baptize a bag of sand as a man,19 if not done in view of the remission of sins and getting of the Holy Ghost.20 Baptism by water is but half a baptism, and is good for nothing without the other half—that is, the baptism of the Holy Ghost.21

The Savior says, “Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.”22 “Though we or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed,” according to Galatians 1:8.23 (July 9, 1843.)

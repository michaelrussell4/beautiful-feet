ITEMS OF INSTRUCTION
Baptism for the Dead

President Joseph Smith, by request of the Twelve Apostles, gave instructions on the doctrine of baptism for the dead,1 which were listened to with intense interest by the large assembly. He presented baptism for the dead as the only way that men can appear as saviors on Mount Zion.2

The proclamation of the first principles of the Gospel3 was a means of salvation4 to men individually; and it was the truth, not men, that saved them;5 but men, by actively engaging6 in rites of salvation substitutionally7 became instrumental in bringing multitudes of their kindred into the kingdom of God.8

Angels and Ministering Spirits

He explained the difference between an angel9 and a ministering spirit;10 the one a resurrected or translated body, with its spirit ministering to embodied spirits—the other a disembodied spirit, visiting and ministering to disembodied spirits. Jesus Christ became a ministering spirit (while His body was lying in the sepulchre) to the spirits in prison,11 to fulfill an important part of His mission, without which He could not have perfected His work,12 or entered into His rest.13 After His resurrection He appeared14 as an angel to His disciples.15

Translated bodies16 cannot enter into rest until they have undergone a change equivalent to death.17 Translated bodies are designed for future missions.

The angel that appeared to John18 on the Isle of Patmos was a translated or resurrected body [i.e., personage]. Jesus Christ went in body after His resurrection, to minister to resurrected bodies. There has been a chain of authority and power from Adam down to the present time.

The best way to obtain truth and wisdom19 is not to ask it from books,20 but to go to God in prayer,21 and obtain divine teaching. It is no more incredible that God should save the dead,22 than that he should raise the dead.23

Pardoning Mercy an Eternal Principle

There is never a time when the spirit is too old24 to approach God.25 All are within the reach of pardoning mercy,26 who have not committed the unpardonable sin,27 which hath no forgiveness,28

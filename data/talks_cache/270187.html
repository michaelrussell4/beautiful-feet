We are glad to hear of the organization of the different quorums in this city, and hope that their organization will be attended to in every stake and branch of the Church, for the Almighty is a lover of order1 and good government.

From the faith and enterprise of the Saints generally, we feel greatly encouraged and cheerfully attend to the important duties devolving upon us,2 knowing that we not only have the approval of Heaven, but also that our efforts for the establishment of Zion and the spread of truth, are cheerfully seconded by the thousands of Israel.3

In conclusion we would say, brethren, be faithful, let your love and moderation be known unto all men;4 be patient, be mindful5 to observe all the commandments6 of your Heavenly Father, and the God of all grace7 shall bless you. Even so. Amen.8—DHC 4:336–339.

JOSEPH SMITH, President

ROBERT B. THOMPSON, Clerk.

The Prophet’s Discourse on Gospel Principles

At 10 o’clock a.m. [May 16, 1841], a large concourse of the Saints assembled on the meeting ground, and were addressed by President Joseph Smith, who spoke at considerable length.

The Doctrine of Agency

He commenced his observations by remarking that the kindness of our Heavenly Father called for our heartfelt gratitude. He then observed that Satan was generally blamed for the evils which we did, but if he was the cause of all our wickedness, men could not be condemned. The devil could not compel mankind to do evil; all was voluntary.9 Those who resisted the Spirit of God,10 would be liable to be led into temptation,11 and then the association of heaven would be withdrawn12 from those who refused to be made13 partakers of such great glory.14 God would not exert any compulsory means,15 and the devil could not; and such ideas as were entertained [on these subjects] by many were absurd. The creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but Christ subjected the same in hope16—all are subjected to vanity while they travel through the crooked paths17 and difficulties which surrounded them.
